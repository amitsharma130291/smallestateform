## Why Most Estate Documents Get Rejected — and How to Avoid It

Families who download a generic estate affidavit and walk into a bank are turned away more often than you'd expect. Here are the four concrete reasons it happens — and why every smallestateform.com document is built to avoid each one:

**1. The document uses a threshold that changed years ago.** Arizona raised its small estate limits in March 2026. Illinois changed theirs in August 2025. California adjusts the personal property threshold annually for inflation. A document downloaded from a generic template site may cite limits from 2019 or 2022 — triggering a bank compliance review that flags the document as potentially outdated and legally unreliable. Every smallestateform.com document reflects current law, not the year the template was last edited.

**2. The form is missing required state-specific language.** Illinois requires a penalty-of-perjury statement. Texas requires specific two-witness attestation language. Arizona requires explicit confirmation of the 30-day waiting period and no-pending-probate status. Generic templates frequently omit these required elements — and a missing element is grounds for rejection, even if everything else is correct.

**3. The document doesn't come with a bank instruction letter.** The single most common reason affidavits are rejected by bank tellers is simple unfamiliarity: the employee has never seen this document before and defaults to "we need a court order." A bank instruction letter citing the exact statute — §14-3971 for Arizona, 755 ILCS 5/25-1 for Illinois, Texas Estates Code Chapter 203 — changes that interaction. It tells the branch manager what the law requires and what refusing a legally sufficient document exposes the bank to. No competitor includes this letter. Every smallestateform.com download does.

**4. County-specific requirements are ignored.** Maricopa County requires different forms than Pima County. Cook County has a completely different threshold formula than the rest of Illinois. A state-level generic template doesn't know which county you're in — and gets the wrong answer as a result. smallestateform.com asks your county and applies the correct rules automatically.
